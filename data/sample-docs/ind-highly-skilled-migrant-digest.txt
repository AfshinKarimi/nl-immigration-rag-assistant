What is a highly skilled migrant permit?
A residence permit to work in the Netherlands as a kennismigrant. You cannot
normally apply for it yourself. A recognised sponsor (the employer) applies
via the IND. The sponsor must already appear on the public register of
recognised sponsors before the application is filed.

Core requirements (high level)
- You meet the general admission requirements that apply to all applicants.
- You have an employment contract (or equivalent appointment) with an employer
  or research institution in the Netherlands that is an IND-recognised sponsor.
- You meet the income (salary) criterion in force on the relevant date.
- The salary must also be in line with the market (comparable jobs). The IND
  may consult UWV if market conformity is unclear.
- Some regulated healthcare professions require BIG registration.

2026 gross monthly salary criterion (excluding holiday allowance)
Published by the IND for applications in 2026 (check the live table before use):
- Highly skilled migrant aged 30 or older: 5,942 euro
- Highly skilled migrant younger than 30: 4,357 euro
- Reduced salary criterion (strict extra conditions): 3,122 euro
- European Blue Card: 5,942 euro
- European Blue Card reduced criterion: 4,754 euro

Salary notes
- Figures are gross per month without holiday pay.
- The amount that applies is usually the one in force on the application date
  for a first permit or an extension with the same employer.
- If you change employer, IND rules look at the amount in force when the new
  contract starts — not necessarily the day the notification is sent.
- Holiday allowance, discretionary bonuses, and irregular pay generally do not
  count the same way as guaranteed monthly salary. The IND looks at SV salary
  (social-insurance wage) on the payslip.

Reduced salary criterion (highly skilled migrant)
IND business information states that the reduced HSM criterion is not automatic.
Typical conditions include: you have or had (or you fully qualify for) the
orientation / search year for highly skilled migrants, and the HSM permit is
applied for within three years of graduation, PhD date, or the end of a
scientific-research permit. The three-year window must not have expired at
application. Confirm the current wording on ind.nl.

Who applies and how long it takes
- The recognised sponsor files the application (often via the IND Business
  Portal).
- Statutory decision time for a regular residence application is commonly
  described as up to 90 days. Recognised-sponsor HSM files are often processed
  faster (IND aims at about two weeks, but missing documents pause the clock).
- If you need an MVV (provisional residence permit / entry visa), the sponsor
  usually applies for MVV and the residence permit together. You collect the
  MVV at the embassy or consulate named in the application.

Fees (2026, IND-published — confirm live table)
- Highly skilled migrant / EU Blue Card residence application: 423 euro
- Recognition as a sponsor (regular): 5,080 euro
- Recognition as a sponsor (reduced, smaller companies — conditions apply):
  2,539 euro
Family-member fees are listed separately on the IND fees pages.

What the permit allows (typical wording)
The residence document for this purpose generally allows work as a highly
skilled migrant for the recognised sponsor. Exact labour-market remarks on the
card are defined by IND; read the brochure 3072 and the sticker/text on your
own permit.

Related but different routes
- EU Blue Card: EU scheme; salary and qualification rules differ (see IND
  Blue Card pages). A degree is typically more central than in the national
  HSM scheme.
- Orientation year (search year) for graduates and researchers: a different
  permit that can later connect to the reduced HSM salary criterion.
- Intra-corporate transferee (ICT): different directive and conditions.
